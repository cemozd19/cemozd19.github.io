Job Market Paper

What Lobbying Buys in Defense R&D: Appropriations, Fielded Systems, and Waste

Each year, Congress funds defense R&D programs that the Department of Defense has asked to end. This paper asks what firm lobbying buys inside this appropriations process, and whether the funding it induces produces fielded weapon systems. I link firms' lobbying on individual R&D programs to program-level budget requests, enacted appropriations, and eventual outcomes, and exploit shocks to firms' congressional connections to identify the effect of lobbying on program survival. A structural model of the review process then separates programs that lobbying carries to a fielded capability from those it keeps alive without producing a system.

Congressional Revolving-Door Staffer Data

Congressional staffers who leave Capitol Hill for registered lobbying firms, linked to the offices and committees they served and to the defense contractors their new firms lobby for.

DoD R&D Project Panel

A project-level panel of Department of Defense R&D programs, built from the annual budget justification documents: requested and enacted funding, milestones and schedules, and how each project evolves over time until it is fielded or terminated.
