Job Market Paper

Appropriations Lobbying and the Fate of Defense R&D Projects: Evidence from Revolving-Door Connections

The Department of Defense (DoD), the largest federal funder of R&D, directs most of that funding to late-stage R&D projects intended to lead to a fielded technology, yet about one in five such projects is terminated. Funding for these projects is decided each fiscal year, and the contractors that perform the work lobby Congress over each year's appropriation. How much funding does that lobbying secure, and does it change which projects terminate and which transition into production? I build a novel dataset of 1,959 late-stage DoD projects, 2004 to 2023, followed from request to outcome, and of their contractors' revolving-door connections, and exploit the arrival of a contractor's lobbyist's former congressional coworker in the personal office of a defense appropriations subcommittee member. A connection raises the firm's annual probability of lobbying by 27 percentage points, from 32 percent, and its projects' funding by 36 percent; over the following four years, the projects it held are terminated 34 percent less often and transition 33 percent more often. I then estimate a dynamic model of R&D projects with lobbying to recover each project's trajectory absent lobbying. The estimates imply that lobbying induced $33 billion of appropriations over these projects' lives, of which $19 billion went to projects that terminate with or without lobbying and $11 billion to projects that lobbying led to transition into a fielded technology.

Congressional Staffer Data

The record of congressional staffers who have served a member of Congress since 1965, with their employment trajectories. Each staffer is linked to the offices and committees they served during their time in Congress.

DoD R&D Project Panel

A project-level panel of late-stage, unclassified Department of Defense R&D projects, built from the annual budget justification documents: requested and enacted funding, milestones and schedules, contractor information, and how each project evolves over time.
