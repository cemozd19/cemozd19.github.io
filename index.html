On the 2026–2027 job market

I am an applied microeconomist and a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Duke University. I study what happens to government-funded R&D, how efficiently it turns public money into innovation, and what policies can improve its allocation. I take these questions to two countries at different stages of R&D development (the United States and Turkey), using administrative data and novel datasets I build from public records.

Lobbying for Innovation? Evidence from Revolving-Door Connections in Defense R&D

Firms can exert influence over the congressional appropriations process that funds public R&D. I study whether lobbying directed at this process affects the allocation and innovation outcomes of publicly funded R&D. To answer this question, I build a dataset of late-stage R&D projects funded by the U.S. Department of Defense from 2004 to 2023 that records their funding, milestones, and whether they successfully completed development, linked to the lobbying activities of the firms that perform them. To address the endogeneity of lobbying, I exploit revolving-door connections: a firm gains political access to a defense appropriator’s office when that office hires a former congressional coworker of the firm’s lobbyists. Within four years of gaining this access, connected firms lobby more often, and Congress appropriates 17 percent more funding for their projects. The share of their projects terminated by the Department of Defense falls by 37 percent and the share completed by about 39 percent, leaving more projects in development without meeting more of their milestones. Political access thus increases funding and prolongs development without progress, a pattern consistent with a rent-seeking view of lobbying.

Data

I share the data used in my research. To request access, please email me at cem.ozdemir@duke.edu.

Congressional Staffer Data

The record of congressional staffers who have served a member of Congress since 1965, with their employment trajectories. Each staffer is linked to the offices and committees they served during their time in Congress.

DoD R&D Project Panel

A project-level panel of late-stage, unclassified Department of Defense R&D projects, built from the annual budget justification documents: requested and enacted funding, milestones and schedules, contractor information, and how each project evolves over time.
